# Living Patterns: Year One editorial plan

The goal is a composed year, not 365 shuffled variations on one idea.

## Editorial balance

Across a rolling 30-episode window, aim for:

- at least 60% materially grounded episodes: rooms, streets, gardens, buildings, objects, thresholds, repair, construction, public space, landscape;
- no more than 20% primarily personal-habit or productivity material;
- 10–20% social, institutional, ecological, or collective applications;
- regular critical episodes that test, limit, complicate, or disagree with an Alexander-derived idea.

Avoid translating every spatial idea into self-help. When an episode begins in material reality, let it stay there when that is where the idea is strongest.

## Twelve loose arcs

These are organizing fields, not rigid monthly syllabi.

1. **Seeing what is already alive** — centers, observation, latent order, repair before replacement.
2. **Scale and nested centers** — levels of scale, strong centers, intermediate structure.
3. **Edges with depth** — boundaries, thresholds, entrances, windows, transitions.
4. **Positive space** — courtyards, rooms, clearings, voids, intervals, pauses.
5. **Paths and movement** — desire lines, sequence, arrival, circulation, gradients.
6. **Making and repair** — craft, maintenance, legible repair, piecemeal growth.
7. **Roughness and local fit** — adaptation, irregularity, tolerances, exceptions.
8. **Participation** — users changing places, feedback, stewardship, shared making.
9. **Public life** — streets, commons, institutions, gathering, conflicting needs.
10. **Nature and climate** — light, water, shade, seasons, ecology, regional fit.
11. **Wholeness under pressure** — standardization, efficiency, abstraction, failure cases.
12. **Return and deepening** — revisit early ideas at a larger scale and with more difficulty.

## Recurrence rules

- Do not repeat the same primary concept within 14 episodes.
- Do not reuse the same concrete example within 30 episodes.
- Revisit important ideas later at a different scale.
- Build deliberate distant echoes: an episode can answer or complicate one published months earlier.
- A recurring idea should deepen, not merely be renamed.

## Ending-form rotation

“A small practice” remains one useful form, not the house template.

Target mix across the year:

- 35% small practice
- 20% observation
- 15% question
- 15% experiment
- 10% invitation to leave something unchanged
- 5% open ending with no instruction

The canonical episode data stores `ending_label`, so the website and feed do not assume one fixed label.

## Critical and counterexample episodes

At least one episode in ten should include friction: a case where an Alexanderian principle conflicts with accessibility, cost, climate, maintenance, privacy, competing users, historical preservation, standardization, or another legitimate need.

The project should investigate these ideas, not venerate them.

## Seasonal composition

Let weather and light matter. Episodes scheduled for a season should occasionally notice what that season actually does to places: summer shade, winter enclosure, rain paths, leaf cover, outdoor social life, heating, cooling, darkness, maintenance cycles.

Avoid making every seasonal reference picturesque.

## Concept metadata

Every episode should carry:

- 2–4 concept tags;
- one concise concept note;
- 1–3 related episode numbers when meaningful;
- an ending form;
- ambience metadata, or an explicit decision to use no ambience.

These fields power the website’s concept browsing and related-reflection links.

## Pre-launch pilot set

Before generating the full year, produce 5–10 private/unlisted pilots spanning:

- one strongly architectural episode;
- one garden or landscape episode;
- one public-space episode;
- one repair/craft episode;
- one critical or counterexample episode;
- at least three ambience families;
- at least three ending forms;
- one episode with no added ambience.

Listen to these in the podcast app across several days. Only then freeze the sonic production lock and write the year.
